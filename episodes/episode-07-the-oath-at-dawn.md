# Episode 7: The Oath at Dawn

**Status:** Complete  
**Word count:** 695 (story only)

## Summary

Slipping down the eastern fissure as dawn breaks, Mara and Fen reach the Stone Hearth just as the scarred captain prepares to choke the tripod forever. Igniting the Hearth saves the northern road, scatters the riders, and binds Mara and Fen to a new oath for what comes next.

## Story

The fissure was barely wide enough for Fen's shoulders, its wet slate slick with frost.

They slid the last ten yards into the shadow of the standing pillars just as the first rim of sun cleared the eastern crags. Behind them, boots clattered among the cairns on the Hill of Crows, too late.

In the center of the granite slab, the scarred captain pulled the red cloth from his clay jar and twisted out the wax stopper. A sharp smell of pitch and iron-salt cut through the morning air.

"Hold the chains taut," the captain ordered his two men.

Fen looked at Mara, his hand tightening on the hem of his coat. There were twenty horses by the outer pillars, and at least a dozen riders still inside the ring. They had no weapons, no bow, and no Calder to draw the patrol away.

Only the needle, burning hot against Mara's ribs.

She unwrapped the scarf.

Even in the grey light of dawn, the brass needle blazed white-gold, bright as molten tin. One of the chained riders gasped and dropped his iron link.

The scarred captain spun around, the clay jar tilted in his hands. "Kill her—not the needle!"

Mara did not run to the side. She ran straight across the granite slab toward the tripod.

A rider lunged from her left, reaching for her cloak. Fen darted low between them, driving his shoulder into the man's knee and sending him sprawling onto the stone. The captain stepped into Mara's path, raising the clay jar like a club to smash her wrist—

Mara threw the brass needle into the tripod's ash-crusted bowl.

For one breathless instant, nothing happened.

Then the white ash shivered.

A chime rang out from the granite beneath their boots—the same deep note Mara had heard in the sunken road-vault, now loud enough to rattle the iron chains. White fire erupted from the tripod, not upward in smoke, but outward in a blinding ring that swept across the slab.

The heatless light struck the clay jar from the captain's hands. It shattered on the granite, its black pitch hissing into harmless steam before it could touch the tripod's rim. Horses reared at the outer pillars, snapping their tethers as the white beam shot straight into the brightening sky.

"Back!" the captain shouted, shielding his scarred face with one forearm. "Mount up! Back to the saddle!"

Within heartbeats, the Stone Hearth emptied in a thunder of hooves and shouted orders. Only the captain lingered at the edge of the pillars, glaring back through the white glare at Mara and Fen.

"You've opened a gate you cannot hold," he called over the wind, before wheeling his pale horse after his men.

When the hoofbeats faded down the ridge, the white fire in the tripod settled into a steady, quiet flame—like the watchtower beacon in Vale Hollow, yet warmer, casting no smoke.

At the bottom of the bowl, unharmed by the fire, lay the brass needle. Its point no longer trembled. It pointed due north, toward the jagged peaks where the sun now struck distant ice.

Far beyond those peaks, carried faintly on the morning wind, came the low answer of a horn—followed by the glint of green banners unfurling along a frozen ridge.

Fen stared northward, shivering in the dawn chill. "What did we just wake?"

Mara picked up the cool brass needle and wrapped it gently in her scarf. Then she took Calder's copper token from her pocket and laid it squarely on the granite threshold of the middle pillar, where anyone coming up from the valleys would see it.

She took her brother's hand. They had left Vale Hollow to save their village, and they had crossed the ford to save themselves. Now the road ahead belonged to neither.

"We keep the northern road open," Mara said quietly, looking toward the distant green banners. "Until Vale Hollow is safe, and until we know whose voice called my name."

"And Calder?" Fen asked.

"He'll find the token," Mara said. "And we'll find the rest."

Together, as full daylight broke over the Stone Hearth, they stepped past the pillars and took the northern path.
