# Episode 3: The Watchers at the Ford

**Status:** Complete  
**Word count:** 742 (story only)

## Summary

At a mist-shrouded river ford north of the pines, Mara and Fen encounter a guarded traveler who knows the mark of the old road. When a quiet signal reveals watchers already waiting across the water, Mara must decide whom to trust before the crossing closes.

## Story

The pines ended above a wide grey river where fog clung to the stones like spilled wool.

Halfway down the bank stood a sunken boundary post, taller than the others Mara had seen. Beneath the circle and three cuts, someone had recently scraped away the moss to leave a fresh notch in the stone.

Fen shivered in his wet coat. "Someone's been here."

"Not long ago," Mara said. She kept the brass needle wrapped in her scarf, though its warmth against her ribs had not faded since the ravine. Ahead, flat stepping stones vanished into the mist.

A branch snapped to their left.

Mara pushed Fen behind the boundary post just as a figure stepped from the alders. He was lean, cloaked in mud-streaked grey, with a short ash bow unstrung across his back and a tin lantern hanging unlit from his belt. His eyes dropped immediately to Mara's scarf.

"You shouldn't carry fire near the water," he said. His voice was raspy, as if he had been breathing cold air for days.

"I'm not carrying fire," Mara answered.

"No?" He tilted his head toward her chest. "Then your ribs are glowing."

Mara looked down. Through the damp wool of her scarf, a pale amber thread of light seeped from the needle. She pressed her hand over it, hiding the glow, though it was already too late.

"Who are you?" she asked.

"Calder," the man said. "And if you're from Vale Hollow, you've brought half the eastern riders behind you."

Fen stepped out beside Mara, chin raised. "We lost them at the ravine."

Calder's gaze flicked toward the pine ridge behind them. "You lost the ones with lanterns. Not the ones who travel dark." He knelt by the water's edge and pointed into the fog. "Look at the far bank. Not at the stones—above them."

Mara squinted through the mist. At first she saw only reeds and wet alder trunks. Then a pale glint shifted among the branches—not a lantern, but the edge of a polished stirrup, catching the first grey light before dawn. Beside it, a second shape stood motionless, watching the ford.

"They didn't follow us from the village," Mara whispered. "They were already ahead."

"Waiting for the beacon to drive you north," Calder said. "The second horn you heard in the trees wasn't a chase. It was a count."

Mara's stomach tightened. In Vale Hollow, the scarred captain had asked for the ember by name, yet the riders had not seemed surprised when she turned north. They had known the road would bring her here.

"Why tell us?" she asked Calder. "If you're one of them, you could call across the river now."

"If I were one of them, I'd have a horse and a dry cloak," Calder replied. He reached inside his collar and pulled out a flat copper token stamped with a circle and three cuts—the exact mark carved into the boundary posts. "My father kept the northern crossings before the road was closed. Tonight the old posts woke up, and someone lit the tower."

Fen stared at the token. "How do we know you didn't take that off someone?"

Calder met the boy's eyes without anger. "You don't. And I don't know whether you're carrying a spark that will get us both killed on the far bank." He rose and slung his bow over one shoulder. "There's a shallow weir half a mile downstream where the stones sit beneath the current. No watchers there—only cold water up to the waist. Wait for full light, and neither crossing will be open."

Behind them, far up the pine ridge, a twig cracked—sharp and deliberate. Not an animal's step. Someone was moving down the slope behind them, closing the bank between the trees and the river.

Mara looked at the mist-covered ford, then at Calder's muddy boots and the copper token hanging against his wool shirt. Trusting a stranger meant walking away from the needle's straight line. Refusing him meant walking into the stirrups waiting across the ford—or staying trapped against the water.

She unwrapped one corner of her scarf. The brass needle swung toward the ford, trembling—then dipped slightly downstream, toward the sound of rushing water over hidden stone.

"Show us the weir," Mara said.

Calder nodded once. "Stay low, and keep that cloth tight."

They slipped into the alders as a shadow detached itself from the pines above the bank, pausing beside the freshly notched boundary post.
