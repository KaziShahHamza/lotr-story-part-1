# Episode 2: The Road Under Rain

**Status:** Complete  
**Word count:** 622 (story only; approximate)

## Summary

Mara and Fen follow the brass needle north through a storm. A flooded ravine and a pursuing scout force them to trust an old road—and reveal that the riders can track the beacon’s fire.

## Story

Rain found them before the hills did.

It swept across the barley fields in silver sheets, turning the northern path to black mud. Mara kept the brass needle wrapped in her scarf. Even through the cloth, its point tugged north.

Fen stumbled behind her. “If this is a road, it’s a poor one.”

“It was a road once.” Mara glanced at the low stone posts half-buried along the path. Each bore the same mark: three short cuts beneath a circle.

Thunder rolled over Vale Hollow. Far behind, a pale flash answered from the watchtower. The beacon still burned.

They reached a ravine at dusk. Water foamed between its banks, swallowing the old stepping stones. On the far side, the path climbed toward a dark line of pines. The needle pulled straight across.

Fen peered into the rain. “We can’t cross that.”

A horn sounded behind them.

Mara turned. A rider stood on the ridge, a small black shape against the grey sky. Another appeared beside him. They had left the road and were searching the hills.

“Down,” Mara whispered.

They crouched beneath the roots of a fallen tree. The riders’ lanterns moved slowly along the ridge. One stopped. Its red light swung toward the ravine, then toward the children’s hiding place.

The needle warmed in Mara’s palm. A faint ember glimmered beneath the brass, answering the distant beacon.

“They’re following that,” Fen breathed.

Mara wrapped the needle tighter. The light dimmed, but the riders began descending the slope.

She searched the bank for a crossing. Beneath the rush of water came a hollow knocking, steady as a heartbeat. Near the ravine’s edge, moss covered a narrow stone arch. A broken sluice gate leaned against it.

“Help me,” she said.

Together they dragged the gate aside. Behind it lay a low culvert, barely wide enough to crawl through. Water ran over its floor, cold around their ankles. Mara went first, keeping one hand on the stone and the other on Fen’s sleeve.

Halfway through, the current shoved her against the wall. The needle slipped from her scarf and spun away into the dark.

“Fen!”

He caught it just before the water carried it beyond reach. For one breath, its point flashed blue-white. The culvert answered with a deep, ancient groan.

Outside, the sluice gate slammed shut behind them. The flood surged through the channel they had just escaped. A shout rose from the ridge, followed by the crash of stone and a horse’s frightened cry.

Mara and Fen crawled out beneath the far bank. They were soaked, bruised, and alive. The riders’ lanterns had vanished from the slope.

The needle’s glow faded. Its point settled north, toward the pines.

Mara looked at the dark water pouring through the ravine. “We keep moving.”

Beyond the trees, a second horn sounded—closer than the first.
