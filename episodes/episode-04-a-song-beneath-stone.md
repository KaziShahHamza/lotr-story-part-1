# Episode 4: A Song Beneath Stone

**Status:** Complete  
**Word count:** 710 (story only)

## Summary

After crossing the river at the submerged weir, Mara, Fen, and Calder take shelter in a sunken road-vault beneath the northern bluffs. There, the brass needle wakes a low resonance in the stonework, revealing a fragment of the road's forgotten purpose—and why the riders are hunting it.

## Story

The weir was colder than the rain had been.

Water climbed to Mara's ribs as she gripped Fen's collar with one hand and felt for mossy stones with her boots. Upstream, the fog held steady over the main ford. No horn blew. No shout followed them. By the time they hauled themselves onto the northern bank, dawn had turned the river the colour of hammered tin.

Calder did not stop at the reeds. He led them up a narrow goat track cut into a limestone bluff, his breath steaming in the grey air. Halfway up, ivy hung thick over a square arch no taller than Mara's shoulder.

"My father called it the dry cellar," Calder said, parting the vines. "Built when the road still carried wagons."

They ducked inside. The air smelled of chalk dust and old cedar. Beyond a short stair lay a vaulted chamber, its floor dry and swept clean by decades of wind. Along the far wall ran three stone alcoves, each carved above its lintel with the same mark Mara had seen on the boundary posts: a circle above three short cuts.

Fen dropped onto a fallen block, teeth chattering. "Why would a road have a cellar?"

"It wasn't a cellar," Mara said slowly.

She unwrapped her scarf. The brass needle had stopped straining toward the river. Now it pointed straight at the middle alcove—and as she stepped closer, a low hum rose from the flagstones beneath her boots.

Calder froze, his hand dropping to the unlit lantern at his belt. "Do you hear that?"

It was not wind. The sound came from the stone itself, a deep, measured vibration like a bow drawn slowly across a thick iron string. Three notes rose, held, and faded, only to begin again.

Mara held the needle near the alcove wall. Where its tip passed, pale blue-white light answered from hairline cracks in the limestone, tracing lines she had never noticed in the dark.

"It's a map," she whispered.

Across the smooth stone back of the alcove, slender grooves caught the needle's glow. She saw Vale Hollow at the southern edge—marked not as a village, but as a watch-lamp. From it, a single line ran north across the ravine and the ford, then split into three branches beneath a jagged ridge. At the northernmost end stood a square symbol beside three parallel strokes.

Beneath the carving, words had been chiselled in plain, angular letters:

*THREE GATES KEEP THE WINTER ROAD. WHEN THE FIRST LAMP WAKES, CARRY THE NEEDLE TO THE STONE HEARTH BEFORE THE RED LANCES CLOSE THE WAY.*

"Red lances," Fen said quietly. "Like the riders in the square."

Calder stepped beside Mara, staring at the glowing grooves. His copper token hung out over his shirt, and for the first time Mara saw that its three cuts matched the three northern branches on the wall.

"My father never told me this was here," Calder murmured. "He only said the token opened the ferryman's lockbox, and that if the tower ever burned white, the northern crossings were dead."

"They aren't dead," Mara said. "They were waiting for someone to read them."

She traced the easternmost branch on the wall. Something else was etched beside the square symbol at the end of the road—a tall, narrow spire with a jagged line beneath it. For a heartbeat, Mara remembered the ember in the watchtower: the black-glass tower, the frozen plain, and the woman's voice calling her name.

The humming in the floor sharpened, rising a half-step in pitch. Dust trickled from the ceiling mortar.

Calder backed toward the stair. "Something's answered it."

"Not the riders," Mara said, though her pulse hammered in her throat. "The stone's warning us."

Outside, beyond the curtain of ivy, hooves struck wet gravel—slow, unhurried, moving along the riverbank below the bluff. Not a charge. A search.

Mara folded the scarf back over the brass needle. Instantly, the blue-white lines on the wall went dark, and the low song beneath their feet sank into silence.

Fen looked from the dark alcove to the ivy-covered arch. "Which of the three branches do we take?"

Mara glanced at Calder's copper token, then up the dark stair toward the ridge road above the bluffs. "The one the riders don't expect."

- The carved map shows Vale Hollow as a watch-lamp, the northern road crossing the ravine and ford, and three northern branches leading toward a jagged ridge and a square "Stone Hearth" symbol (accompanied by an etching resembling the black-glass spire from Mara's Episode 1 vision).

- Calder's copper token (circle and three cuts) corresponds to the three northern branches; his father never told him about the chamber's map.
