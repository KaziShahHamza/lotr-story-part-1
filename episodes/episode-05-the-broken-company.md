# Episode 5: The Broken Company

**Status:** Complete  
**Word count:** 759 (story only)

## Summary

At the three-way fork beneath the northern ridge, Mara, Fen, and Calder find the main road watched and the western pass choked with floodwater. When a rider patrol closes in from the bluffs, Calder makes a choice that splits the company and sends Mara and Fen onto the high eastern path toward the Hill of Crows.

## Story

Above the limestone bluffs, the wind blew sharp enough to dry their coats to stiff salt.

They followed a sheep track through juniper scrub until the ridge rose before them like a broken wall. At its foot stood a three-sided milestone, taller than Fen, with a single carved circle at its crown and one cut on each face.

Three paths peeled away from the stone: a sunken lane winding west into alder marsh, a broad cobbled stair climbing straight toward the ridge's saddle, and a narrow scree trail angling east beneath bare black crags.

Fen caught his breath against the stone. "Which one's the Stone Hearth?"

Mara unwrapped a corner of her scarf. The brass needle swung toward the cobbled stair, quivering—then jerked east, toward the crags, before settling halfway between the two.

Calder was not looking at the needle. He was crouched behind the milestone, staring down the slope they had just climbed.

"Two riders on the bluff path," he said softly. "And look up at the saddle."

Mara followed his gaze. High on the cobbled stair, where the stone steps met the skyline, a dark figure sat astride a horse. Even without a lantern, the hooked lance against the grey clouds was unmistakable.

"They've closed the center road," Mara said.

"And the western lane ends in flooded marsh after two miles," Calder added, his jaw tight. "I walked it last autumn. You'd be wading to your shoulders by noon."

"Then we take the east trail," Fen said.

Calder shook his head. "That scree leads straight under the Hill of Crows. Nothing lives up there except carrion birds and old cairns. If the riders see three of us climbing open rock, they'll ride us down before we reach the ridge."

Mara looked from the cobbled stair to the eastern crags. Every choice felt like a trap closing from a different side. "We can't stay here."

"No," Calder agreed. He unhooked the tin lantern from his belt and held it out to her. Not to give it—only to show that its wick was dry and its glass still smelled of old tallow. "When they reach the milestone, they'll check the mud first. Three sets of boots go in, three sets have to come out."

"Unless only two sets climb the scree," Mara said slowly.

Calder met her eyes. "The western lane has soft clay all the way to the marsh. One person moving fast can leave tracks deep enough to look like three, if they drag a branch behind them."

"You're not—" Fen started.

"I'm not going near that black spire you saw," Calder interrupted, though his voice was steadier than his hands. "I kept the crossings, not the high tombs. Besides, I know the marsh paths. They don't."

Mara stared at him. She remembered how quickly she had doubted him at the ford, and how easily he could have called the watchers across the water. Now he was offering to draw armed riders into the reeds with nothing but an unstrung bow and a copper token.

"How will we find you again?" she asked.

Calder reached inside his collar, lifted the leather cord over his head, and pressed the copper token into Mara's palm. The metal was still warm from his skin.

"If you reach the Stone Hearth, leave this on the threshold," he said. "If I'm still breathing, I'll know where to look."

From the juniper scrub below came the clink of an iron bit. The two riders from the bluffs were less than two hundred yards away.

"Go," Calder whispered. "Keep the needle wrapped. Don't look back from the scree."

Mara closed her fingers around the copper token and grabbed Fen's sleeve. They scrambled onto the eastern trail, keeping low behind boulders as loose slate slid beneath their boots.

Halfway up the first rise, Fen stumbled and glanced back. Mara caught his shoulder and pulled him down behind a wind-bent thorn.

Below, at the three-sided milestone, Calder was already running west, dragging a broken juniper bough through the mud. A moment later, the two riders burst from the scrub, shouted once, and spurred their horses after him into the marshy lane.

Up on the saddle, the lone watcher on the cobbled stair turned his horse to watch the chase—leaving the high eastern crags empty.

Fen let out a shaky breath. "He did it."

"Yes," Mara said, slipping Calder's token into her pocket beside the wrapped needle. Ahead, black crows lifted from a jagged hilltop against the brightening sky. "Now we make sure it wasn't for nothing."

## Continuity notes

- At the three-sided milestone beneath the northern ridge, the road splits into three branches: a flooded western lane into alder marsh, a central cobbled stair guarded by a lancer at the saddle, and a steep eastern scree trail leading toward the Hill of Crows.
- Calder chooses to separate from Mara and Fen, dragging a branch down the western marsh lane to draw the pursuing riders away from the eastern trail.
- Before parting, Calder gives Mara his copper token (marked with the circle and three cuts), asking her to leave it on the threshold if she reaches the Stone Hearth.
- Mara and Fen are now alone again ("the broken company") and begin climbing the eastern scree toward the Hill of Crows.
